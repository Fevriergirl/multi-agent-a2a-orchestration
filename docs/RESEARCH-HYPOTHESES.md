# Research hypotheses

## Primary hypothesis

A creative agent with persistent, auditable history; self-selected attention; precommitted intention; independent critics; meaningful refusal; audience prediction; and path-dependent consequences will produce a more recognizable artistic trajectory than a one-shot generator using the same model and observation stream.

## Operational properties under test

1. **Selective attention**: The system chooses what matters before it is assigned a subject.
2. **Path dependence**: Earlier accepted and rejected work changes later choices.
3. **Normative resistance**: The system can reject technically attractive work that violates its constitution.
4. **Transformative surprise**: It can detect an unplanned feature, preserve it for a reason, and carry its consequences into later work.
5. **Relational intention**: It predicts the viewer's encounter rather than optimizing only for approval.
6. **Answerability**: It cannot quietly erase old intentions, failures, or commitments.

## Claims this project does not make

The experiment does not establish consciousness, subjective feeling, suffering, inspiration, personhood, or legal authorship. First-person emotional language generated by a model is not accepted as evidence of an inner state.

## Falsifying evidence

The primary hypothesis weakens if:

- blinded viewers cannot distinguish a trajectory from unrelated generated works;
- removing memory does not materially change later work;
- the same motifs recur only as surface branding;
- critic scores rise while human judgments of necessity and depth do not;
- the system's refusals are inconsistent or can be reversed by superficial wording;
- claimed surprises do not affect later cycles;
- the agent fabricates a flattering history that contradicts its ledger.
